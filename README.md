# Content Analysis: How Content Spreads Online

## Summary 
Analysis of how a piece of content spreads beyond its original context: examining where it gets used, referenced, discussed across different online platforms. 
The analysis was conducted as part of an analytical test task focused on transforming unstructured data into a structured dataset and identifying patterns in content usage. The selected piece of content was the New York Times article "The New Fabio Is Claude" which discusses the use of AI in writing romance books. 

## Research question 
How the article is used and interpreted across platforms? 

## Data collection 
Data collection was performed manually across multiple platforms. Sample: 12 sources 

## Methodology 
A structured dataset was created in a spreadsheet and a classification framework was applied to all collected sources.

### Classification Categories
1. Content usage: Reference, Discussion, Reuse of ideas, Retelling
2. Platform
3. Content type: Long text, Short text, Video
4. Position toward article (NYT): Positive, Neutral, Negative
5. Position toward AI-generated books: Positive, Neutral, Negative
6. Referenced elements: Statistics, Quotes from the author, Quotes from publishers, Author’s business, Title of the article, AI writing process details
7. Purpose of use: Critique of article/media, Critique of AI/author, Support for AI, Informing, Strategic use, Broader discussion

Additionally, selected elements of the SWOT framework were used to identify perceived:
1. Threats
2. Opportunities
related to AI-generated books.

## Key findings 
1. Platform differences
- Reddit communities focused on AI writing tended to interpret the article positively and strategically.
- Long-form platforms (e.g., Substack, Medium) were more likely to discuss media framing and criticize the article itself. 

2. Content usage patterns

The most common use case was discussion. The article was frequently used for:
- debates about ethics of AI
- criticism of author or the article itself
- support for AI-related arguments

3. Negative position towards article

Both pro-AI and anti-AI users criticized the NYT article but they did it differently:
-  pro-AI users mostly criticised manipulation of facts
- anti-AI users mostly criticised not providing evidence in article and for the lack of skepticism towards AI-generated books

4. Referenced content
- Statistics and quotes of the author were the most referenced content of the article
- Different communities relied on different parts of the article depending on their position toward AI
